### Sports: fixed-window WNBA connection and executable study

The fixed scope was one league, 2026-07-27 through 2026-08-02 UTC. The authorized manifest contained three non-empty WNBA state objects totaling 46,682 bytes and 861 rows. They cover six distinct provider games, so all six form the frozen list (below the cap of 20), sorted by official scheduled start UTC and then provider ID. Official WNBA game pages establish the corresponding schedule: IND@POR at 2026-08-01 02:00Z, LV@CHI at 17:00Z, NY@PHX at 19:00Z, IND@MIN at 2026-08-02 17:00Z, LA@POR at 19:30Z, and CONN@DAL at 23:00Z. Team/date/home-away and final score agree for all six. This is a manually audited identity join; the provider `game_id` is not a Polymarket key.

Five of six games have an explicit `HT` row and five have terminal rows. The first frozen game has one terminal row and no explicit halftime state, so it remains a failed denominator and receives no inferred decision time. The second has first explicit halftime receipt at 18:02:14.027Z and a preregistered decision time 30 seconds later. Polymarket metadata verifies that both first candidates have full-game, two-outcome moneyline contracts. Their rules name the two teams, include overtime in the final result, keep postponed games open, and resolve a fully canceled game 50/50; spreads, totals, props, and replacement lines are excluded. Thus candidate 1 connects schedule, provider identity, contract, rules, and settlement but fails the halftime decision-state requirement. Candidate 2 connects those layers plus the decision state; it fails the bounded quote join: neither the 18 UTC object nor the adjacent 17 UTC lookback object contains a record for either exact moneyline token. The extra lookback was registered before reading, within the two-hour Sports/four-hour total limit, without changing the game, tokens or decision.

The state source exposes collector receipt time, provider update time, score, period, elapsed, live/ended state, and terminal score, but no odds. These clocks must remain distinct from official schedule time and CLOB receive/source time. The analysis clock is the collector receipt: use the latest state received at or before first explicit `HT` receipt plus 30 seconds. Never infer halftime from Q2, Q3, or the final row. For the CLOB object, the outer timestamp is the receive clock; parse the content array, select messages whose inner event type is `price_change`, expand their changes, then select the two exact moneyline tokens. Per token, use the latest raw record at or before the decision without replacing an invalid latest record with an earlier valid one. The originally specified inclusive finite [0,1] / bid<=ask arithmetic check is retained as a diagnostic; a usable two-sided quote requires strict 0<bid<ask<1 with no latest-time conflict. Snapshot tops are diagnostic_unvalidated and do not establish a connection. Exact identifiers and rows stay private.

The proposed prediction target is the final winner at the first received explicit halftime-end state plus 30 seconds. The question is whether halftime score differential improves held-out final-win probability after conditioning on the simultaneously observed Polymarket moneyline probability. This asks whether the market already compresses the public scoreboard state; it is not an execution or profit claim.

Direct overlap exists with published basketball win-probability research: McFarlane (2019, DOI `10.3233/JSA-180231`) models NBA win probability using time remaining, score differential, possession, and pregame point spread; Maddox, Sides, and Harvill (2022, arXiv `2204.11777`) model college-basketball in-game home-win probability with lead differential, elapsed time, and a time-weighted pregame probability. These support a score-state plus prior-probability baseline. This pilot did not complete a systematic literature review of prediction-market-conditioned WNBA halftime models, so field-level novelty remains unknown.

The smallest valid comparison is:

- M0: logistic recalibration of the simultaneous two-token moneyline probability using training folds only.
- M1: the same model plus signed halftime score differential. Do not add possession, injuries, pace, or a team prior unless a later audit freezes those fields before outcome access.
- One row per game; forward split by complete game date, with all data from one game kept together. Never treat tokens or markets from one game as independent observations.
- Primary evaluation: held-out date-equal log loss, reported as `LL(M0)-LL(M1)` with paired game/date uncertainty. Brier score and reliability are secondary. No threshold trading, return, or reaction-speed evaluation.

The current two-game attempt is schema feasibility and must not be fitted. A launchable cohort needs official schedule identity, one full-game moneyline contract and rule set, an explicit halftime receipt, a two-token decision-time book with a declared age rule, and the official final outcome for every retained game. Stop if identity is ambiguous, explicit halftime is materially missing and only inferable, historical books lack a trustworthy receive clock or two-token coverage, contract/outcome identity is not one-to-one, or a frozen multi-date cohort cannot support forward held-out evaluation. Stop M1 if primary held-out date-equal log loss does not improve or the direction is unstable across forward dates.
