# Rocklabs BTC 5-minute trade identity and book replay diagnostic

**Decision, 2026-09-24:** `BLOCKED_SUPPLIER_SEMANTICS`. The July 28 pilot gives a strong *transaction-to-matching-group* link and 24 independently checked structural examples. It does not establish a bounded matching time for each passive maker leg or an exact, receive-causal L2 book at that time. The two preregistered July 29–30 measurement windows reproduce the structural link for most prints, but show materially more unjoined prints and nonzero best-price, touch-size and full-book replay failures. The original Quote-or-Pull action study remains `STOP / DATA_GATED`; this batch authorizes no new model, strategy result, cancellation-value estimate, or final-cohort claim.

## Why this diagnostic was run

The original research needs three distinct facts: which *actual passive maker legs* belong to a CLOB trade, when those legs matched within a justified error bound, and what exact-token book a decision maker could have observed before the earliest possible match. Rocklabs supplies CLOB prints, book snapshots and level changes, plus Polygon fill and match logs. These fields make the question testable, but their mere presence is not the required timing or replay guarantee.

The [Quant Application source audit](https://github.com/DeepCogNeural/Quant-Application/blob/fbafd8da289d00e5205660e311c1aa137ab1ca40/portfolio/open_source/rocklabs-qop-data-audit-20260924.md) registered 2026-07-28 08:00–09:00 UTC as the initial development sample and 2026-07-29 and 07-30 at the same UTC hour as `DEVELOPMENT_MEASUREMENT_VALIDATION` before reading their content. These are three exposed development hours, not an independent final sample. Their eight additional source files were checked against the manifest and SHA-256 values in [validation_sources.json](../results/rocklabs_qop_identification_v1/validation_sources.json). The original four file hashes and the mechanism, comparison units, 24-example selection rule, resource limits and stop conditions are in [diagnostic_manifest.json](../results/rocklabs_qop_identification_v1/diagnostic_manifest.json). The frozen replay rule is [replay_rule_v1.json](../results/rocklabs_qop_identification_v1/replay_rule_v1.json). Later script edits added validation-window input hash checks and did not alter the replay update or comparison rule.

The source ZIP and raw hourly data, signed URLs, full transaction/receipt examples, account and order identifiers, and row-level mismatches stay under gitignored `_private/`. Public artifacts contain aggregates and source/evidence hashes only. The 07-28 ZIP and all eight 07-29/30 objects passed local ClamAV scanning; the scanner result is a file-handling check, not a proof of harmlessness or data validity.

## 1. CLOB prints to real maker fills

The parser expands array-valued CLOB messages, identifies BTC-5m tokens from embedded market metadata, joins a print's `transaction_hash` to same-hour Polygon logs, and splits `OrderFilled` batches at each `OrdersMatched`. A group must have the expected Polygon chain, observed exchange contract and event signatures; exactly one fill matching `takerOrderHash`; and matching taker token, side, maker, amount and CLOB print share quantity. Remaining event-derived legs are keyed by `(chain, contract, tx, log_index)` and their price/shares/direction derive from the exchanged assets, including complementary-token routes. Reused order hashes are **not** treated as new unique orders or as uniquely identifying a leg. This is a sample-specific event reconciliation, not a claim that an ABI alone certifies all transactions. The observed exchange source/ABI are explorer verified, but a sample-block bytecode hash was not independently obtained; see [contract_abi_audit.json](../results/rocklabs_qop_identification_v1/contract_abi_audit.json).

No target transaction hash or joined matching group was duplicated by CLOB print records in these three hours. July 29 had 483 prints with no same-hour matching logs and one with logs but no qualifying unique print-to-group match; July 30 had 683 without logs. These cross-date gaps have **not** been independently receipt-audited and must not be called reverted transactions. In the July 28 fixed initial sample, all three unjoined print transaction hashes were checked individually against Polygon receipts: each transaction failed and had no matching fill log. This narrows the cause there, but the supplier must explain why failed/provisional hashes appear as `last_trade_price` prints.

The preselected July 28 independent audit checked six each of single-maker, multi-maker, complementary-token and reused-order cases (24 distinct transactions), using decoded input and complete receipt logs; all 24 passed the specified structural reconciliation. Thirty-seven complementary-route maker inputs were among 41 maker inputs checked. The fixed-example result supports the mechanism but does **not** independently validate every one of the 15,346 candidate legs or the 07-29/30 legs. ## 2. Can the book be replayed at the decision time?

From each `book` snapshot, the fixed parser applies intervening `price_change` entries by exact token, Decimal price and *absolute* size; zero deletes the level. It expands array messages and resets at the next snapshot. Both file order and stable exchange-millisecond sorting are diagnostic comparisons; only the former retains recorded receive order, and even that ordering is not yet guaranteed to reconstruct the exchange's complete causal stream. Exchange sorting can move later-received updates into earlier state. It is **not** a live feature construction rule.

The first chronological July 28 event exactly reproduces the earlier full-depth baselines: **1,495/2,229** comparable transitions in file order and **1,883/2,229** under stable exchange-time sorting. The same unchanged rule applied to the other seven identified events and then the two registered dates gives:

Exact numeric normalization rules out JSON key or string formatting as the whole explanation. In the initial event, one decisive adjacent-snapshot example remains wrong at **57 bid and 35 ask levels, including the best prices**, after 442 intervening updates under stable exchange sorting. Across all July 28 target tokens, exchange sorting still leaves 139 best-price and 2,664 full-book failures. It also reverses receive order for 104,384 adjacent records in that hour (407,184 and 734,204 on the two validation dates). Numerous same-millisecond conflicting level sizes, snapshots sharing a millisecond with updates, and identical repeated updates are recorded in the public replay summaries. None of these observations independently identifies the correct order, the snapshot reset rule, or missing stream events. The data supplier has been asked for the sequence, snapshot, merge/recovery and `hash` semantics with the minimal counterexample.

`price_change.best_bid/best_ask` are present as numeric fields in most target updates, and 683,261/720,779, 3,807,828/4,002,532 and 6,029,485/6,182,485 respective file-order update observations satisfy the simple `0 < bid < ask < 1` check. Presence and arithmetic validity do **not** prove that these values are an exact, timestamp-causal decision book, include touch quantities, or represent the state just before a maker match. They are a candidate for a separately defined receive-time BBO-only study after source semantics and coverage are settled. No future snapshot was used to pick an update ordering, repair a book, or infer actionability.

## 3. Matching-time evidence

July 28 has 7,179 joined economic groups and 15,346 event-derived maker legs; 2,497 groups have multiple maker legs. The public CLOB print contributes one nested millisecond timestamp per transaction, not an independent timestamp for each leg. The collector's outer receipt comes later by 2–1,480 ms (median 12 ms, p95 159 ms). Polygon block time is whole-second and its indexer receive time is later; neither bounds the unknown interval between matching and the print timestamp. A shared narrow interval for all legs in one group would be sufficient *if* its generating mechanism or independent records supported a maximum error. No such upper bound was found in this dataset or the published schema. [clock_aggregate.json](../results/rocklabs_qop_identification_v1/clock_aggregate.json) therefore remains `UNKNOWN_PER_LEG_MATCH_TIME`. We do not use network delay or a block-time difference as a surrogate error bound.

The previously declared QOP gate requires a defensible interval with error at most `min(500 ms, actionable lead / 4)`, and inputs available before the earliest possible match. This batch cannot satisfy that gate. The supplier follow-up asks where `last_trade_price.timestamp` is generated and its worst-case offset or group duration.

## 4. Separate B probability-study input coverage

The prior B experiment sampled the Up-token quote at event end minus 120 seconds, required a receive-time BBO age of at most 1,000 ms, and verified the final payout label. These three Rocklabs hours contain 32 active BTC-5m events, 64 active tokens, many `book` and direct BBO fields, and the event/token identities used above. However, **none of the 64 active token IDs appears in the accompanying daily `polymarket_index.token_index` or `onchain_index.token_to_condition` files** (0/16, 0/22, 0/26 by date). The on-chain indexes were generated around midnight *after* each sampled day; they cannot be treated as point-in-time predictor fields. This batch did not establish Up-token mapping, a verified end-minus-120-second quote at the exact B rules, or payout labels through a later resolution source. Verified event rows satisfying **all** B requirements: **0 established**, rather than 0 proven to exist. This is a coverage audit only: no B fit, score or transfer claim was run. A future B multi-date cohort needs independently matched identities and labels, fixed decision-time quotes and age/conflict handling, and source-consistent preprocessing before any frozen model comparison.

## Decision and handoff

**A. Maker-leg identity:** uniquely indexed event-derived legs for joined July 28 transactions, with 24 independently verified structural examples; full-population independent transaction-input verification and cross-date unjoined causes remain open. **B. Book:** neither full depth nor touch size is certified causally; best prices still fail some snapshot checks, including under noncausal offline sorting. **C. Clock:** independent maker-match interval bound is `UNKNOWN`. **D. QOP:** `STOP / DATA_GATED`; no new frozen action measurement or cancellation-value evaluation. **E. Probability B:** data fields exist, but a multi-date, labeled fixed-decision table is not ready.

The next falsifiable evidence is the Rocklabs answer to the three emailed questions: print/maker-match time semantics and bound; snapshot/update ordering, completeness and `hash`; and failed transaction prints. If those answers give a source-backed deterministic rule, register a *new* replay and clock protocol before testing it on genuinely unexposed hours. If not, stop this source path for the original action study. Storage of selected raw files is an independent provenance task and does not change any scientific gate.
