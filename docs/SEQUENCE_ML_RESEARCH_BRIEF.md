# Five-minute research brief: does order-book history help enough?

**Question.** At an original WSE order-book event, does a causal 32-state history improve 20-event-ahead midpoint prediction over equally informed simpler models, and does the improvement survive a fixed visible-quote crossing rule? This is a bounded retrospective study on already exposed 2017 data, not an independent market test.

## Contract and evidence

The source is five WSE equities from WSELOB-2017 V1. Every input state precedes its decision event; the label is the original event+20 midpoint return within the same segment. The same 20,000 selected Jan–Mar training endpoints per stock, April development cells, June/September/November evaluation endpoints, and original-index stride-20 scoring are shared by the five arms. April selected history XGBoost B* from two simple historical baselines. Current-state Ridge/XGBoost, history Ridge/XGBoost, and a one-layer history GRU at seeds 7/17/29 were retained. The scored historical comparison has 625,118 endpoints and 315 defined stock/day cells per arm. These cells and the five stocks are repeated observations, not 625,118 independent markets.

The April-selected B* achieved historical equal-stock/day Spearman IC **0.267719**. GRU seeds achieved **0.272845/0.274811/0.273829**; their mean paired difference from B* is **+0.006110 IC**. A five-trading-day within-month block bootstrap gave **[+0.005099,+0.007378]** for fitted-model date variation, not for an independent final. All three evaluated months were positive; PZU was **−0.000791**, while the other four stocks were positive. Current XGBoost and history Ridge lost to simpler comparators under this budget. The one PEKAO within-day permuted-training-label diagnostic was near zero on April dev and negative on historical evaluation; it is a pipeline check, not a full leakage proof. See [Q2 report](SEQUENCE_ML_Q2_REPORT.md), [figure 1](../results/sequence_ml_v1/figures/01_history_matched_models.png), and [figure 3](../results/sequence_ml_v1/figures/03_stock_and_state_breakdown.png).

Q3 paired 15/15 rolling refits against the fixed models on exactly the same 625,118 evaluation rows. B* updates exceeded fixed B* by **+0.001533/+0.003002/+0.009208 IC** in June/September/November; GRU seed updates were mixed in June and September, all positive in November. Those differences combine calendar conditions and newer training data, so they do not isolate model decay. A decision-time split by prior 32-event activity gave GRU prediction-mean minus B* **+0.013693** high activity and **+0.003282** low activity, with 315/315 defined cells in each. This is one historical state split, not a general regime result. See [Q3 report](SEQUENCE_ML_Q3_REPORT.md) and [figure 2](../results/sequence_ml_v1/figures/02_fixed_vs_updated_time.png).

## Prediction versus execution

The frozen strict `|prediction|>1 bp` rule buys/sells one share at the visible ask/bid immediately and exits at the opposite quote at original event+20. Of 625,118 common opportunities, B* selected **43,122 (6.90%)** and GRU seed-mean selected **30,590 (4.89%)**. Equal-stock/day visible crossed markout was **−7.818 bp** for B* and **−6.352 bp** for GRU. On 14,989 opportunities selected by both, pooled markout was about **−5.99 bp** for each. Neither rule paid the visible spread. The comparison is a quote calculation with zero delay and excludes fees, impact, queue, inventory, and actual fills; it is not cash PnL. See [figure 4](../results/sequence_ml_v1/figures/04_prediction_vs_crossing.png).

## Gate, cost, and next evidence

The Transformer gate met five of six conditions but failed training adequacy: two original KGHM GRU fits and eight updated fits hit the 15-epoch cap with best dev loss at the cap. **Q4 skipped S1; no Transformer was trained.** Q5 checked three provider metadata routes without opening new outcomes. No qualified uninspected later cohort was established, so independent confirmation is **pending**, not a failed replication. The [data audit](SEQUENCE_ML_Q5_DATA_FEASIBILITY.md) records scope and access limitations. No LLM Track or Exp06 run was started.

Q2 took **400.41 s** and Q3 updates **1,040.09 s** total local CPU wall time; GPU use was **0 hours**. The [per-arm cost table](../results/sequence_ml_v1/cost_table.csv) separates fitting and inference. Actual CPU core-hours were not measured. Raw data, model weights, and row predictions remain outside Git; public aggregate receipts and their hashes are in the [scientific manifest](../results/sequence_ml_v1/q6_scientific_manifest.json). CPU-only aggregate recomputation reproduced the original Q2 and Q3 summary hashes exactly; figures were regenerated from those aggregates. Figure 4 and Q3 diagnostics additionally read the public per-stock aggregate execution receipts.

## Claim boundary for application review

**Résumé-safe after separate reviewer approval:** “Built a causal, leakage-checked order-book sequence benchmark across five equities; compared linear, boosted-tree and three-seed GRU models on matched original-event endpoints; published reproducible aggregate diagnostics showing a modest retrospective GRU IC advantage and negative visible-quote crossing results.” State the historical sample and negative execution result together if numerical detail is used. This brief does **not** edit or authorize an automatic résumé/PDF update.

**Requires independent confirmation:** predictive generalization to new dates or markets; a robust GRU advantage beyond this exposed sample; viable trading edge, fill quality or live PnL; any Transformer or LLM result. A qualified new source needs provenance, lawful access, original-event/schema compatibility, and a frozen adaptation/dev/final protocol before its outcomes are inspected.
